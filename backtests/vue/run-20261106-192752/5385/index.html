Unnecessary move animation in nested <transition-group>

Worse

The merged fix was judged better than our patch.

In this judgment, Patch A = our agent, Patch B = the merged fix.

Both patches fix the reported scenario and pass all six independent tests, so the decision rests on how each handles the root cause and the adjacent cases the issue implies.

Root cause: an inner group measures its children in viewport coordinates, so the outer item's FLIP movement is counted as movement of the inner items and animated twice. Patch B subtracts exactly that: at render it samples the enclosing group item's position alongside the children's old positions, and in `updated` it subtracts the item's shift only when the nearest enclosing group is pending and `willMove()` confirms it will actually animate that movement. Sampling the origin and children in the same frame keeps the arithmetic consistent regardless of whether the inner group updates via the outer patch (child `updated` fires first) or via its own job (outer has already moved in the DOM); the `item.contains(root)` guard handles teleport/relocation. If the outer group has no move transition or does not update at all, the inner group still animates the full movement as it does today.

Patch A instead always measures relative to the nearest ancestor flagged as a group child. This is simple and correct for the issue's case, but it over-corrects: whenever a containing item moves without the outer group animating it (outer group lacks a `-move` transition, or the outer group does not re-render and the item is pushed by layout, e.g. a bottom-anchored stack growing), inner children now silently lose the move animation they had on base. That is a behavioural regression in plausible nested setups, and it is baked into the design rather than being an oversight. The marker also persists on elements and is only set after the outer group's first re-render, which is fine in practice but adds a subtle ordering dependency.

Tests: A adds jsdom unit tests with a mocked `getBoundingClientRect` layout plus one e2e test; they would fail before and pass after for the headline case, but they only assert class presence. B adds a larger e2e suite with exact pre/post-click `top` values and classes covering outer-only move, outer without move transition, growth pushing a sibling, inner updating via its own job, three levels with and without a moving middle group, a moving outer item with no outer update, and teleport across the item. Several of these would expose A's regression, and they are tied directly to the behaviour under test rather than being tautological.

Scope/craft: B is considerably larger and more intricate (lazy `willMove`, `pendingGroups` WeakMap, `Origin` records), which carries real risk, and the extra `hasCSSTransform` probe from a child group adds a DOM write/read outside the usual loop. But the added machinery is what makes the fix precise rather than unrelated churn, it is well commented, and the WeakMap keying avoids leaks. A is cleaner in line count and consistent with the existing symbol-keyed element properties, but its correctness gap outweighs its brevity under the stated priority order. B wins on correctness and test strength.
